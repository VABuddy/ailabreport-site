ChatGPT Planned Our Fishing Trip. Here’s What Still Needed a Human.

The goal was Andrew’s first rainbow trout. ChatGPT organized the weekend; a route check, flexible choices and generous anglers made it work.

I used ChatGPT to plan a fishing weekend with my son Andrew. We had one priority: his first rainbow trout. That was a much better starting point than asking for a list of attractions.

The resulting plan gave us places to try, a weekend schedule, gear to think about and a backup. It also gave us a reminder of what an AI itinerary cannot do: confirm every changing condition, judge a route the way you would, or put a fish on the line.

Watch the complete father-son fishing episode for the original trip footage and planning conversation.

Start with the outcome that matters

During planning, a waterfall hike sounded appealing until we considered how much of the day it would take. I pushed back: we were there to fish, especially for rainbow trout. That clarification narrowed the itinerary.

For a family trip, give the assistant the constraints it cannot infer: dates, starting region, shore-fishing needs, your child’s experience, equipment and budget. Say which goal should win when activities compete for time. Ask for a printable version if someone else will help follow the plan; I wanted Andrew to be our fishing reporter.

A useful plan makes uncertainty visible

Before we left, ChatGPT said it could not confirm that stocking had happened. That was useful information. An itinerary can suggest a promising place while still lacking the evidence you need before travelling.

For your own visit, use the Virginia Department of Wildlife Resources stocking reports and official fishing information to check the relevant rules and licenses. Confirm access and hours with the responsible land manager. A previous trip or stocking report does not promise present conditions or a catch.

The Friday route needed a second opinion

Our weekend base was Harrisonburg. Friday’s plan included dinner on the road, Walmart and the hotel. Traffic was slow, and the dinner stop took us through Richmond. The stop worked; the route was not ideal for us.

My change for next time is straightforward: check the entire journey in a navigation app before committing to a restaurant. A sensible stop considered on its own can still be an awkward detour. Ask the AI for its reasoning, then inspect the route and travel time yourself.

ChatGPT chose a place. People helped Andrew catch his first trout.

Saturday started at Hone Quarry. The mountain drive and lake looked great, but we had been fishing for about forty-five minutes without a bite. Nearby anglers were catching fish, so I asked what they were using.

One man made room for Andrew and let him use his rod. A few minutes later, Andrew caught his first trout. Before leaving, the man gave him a rod and the bait he had left. Andrew shook his hand and thanked him. That rod became his favorite.

That is the distinction I want to keep: ChatGPT helped us choose somewhere to go. The people beside the water helped make the moment happen.

Keep the backup—and permission to change dinner

Briery Branch gave us another option. Andrew was unsure about the walk down, but he was glad we tried it. We fished both lakes and returned to Hone Quarry after Andrew picked his favorite. The itinerary had prepared choices without taking away our ability to choose.

Dinner changed too. The plan said steak; Andrew wanted hibachi. We made a reservation at Kyoto and enjoyed the evening. He named the two trout we kept Dinner and Dinnerbone, which got people at the table laughing.

Sunday left time for another morning by the water, followed by Buc-ee’s and the journey home. Andrew’s recorded weekend total was eight trout on Saturday and one more on Sunday. That is our trip’s outcome, not a result another itinerary can guarantee.

A planning request you can adapt

Plan a shore-fishing weekend for a parent and child using these dates, starting region, experience, equipment and budget. Prioritize the child’s first trout. Compare practical options, include a backup, show the full travel route, and flag anything you cannot verify. Separate suggestions from facts I need to check before leaving.

Before using the plan, check five things:

  • The full route, including meal and shopping detours.
  • Current rules, licenses, stocking information and access.
  • Whether the walking, timing and equipment suit your child.
  • A realistic backup if the first option disappoints.
  • Enough room to change the plan without spoiling the weekend.

ChatGPT was useful because it organized the choices. Patience, Andrew’s enthusiasm and other people’s kindness did the rest. The best part for me was being out there with him.
