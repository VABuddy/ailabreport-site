2026 World Cup: Can AI Predict the Future of Football?

We challenged four of the most powerful AI systems on the planet to predict the 2026 FIFA World Cup, and the results were anything but unanimous.

Football, or soccer as some know it, is a game of passion, skill, and unpredictable outcomes. But what if artificial intelligence could cut through the noise and tell us who would lift the 2026 FIFA World Cup trophy? We put this question to the four leading AI systems: Gemini, ChatGPT, Claude, and Grok. The prompt was identical, the tournament data was the same, and what came back was not what we expected.

The Experiment: AI vs. The Beautiful Game

The 2026 FIFA World Cup is set to be the biggest tournament in history, with 48 teams competing across North America. As the group stage concludes and the Round of 32 begins, we wanted to know if the world's best AI could truly predict the champions. So, we designed an experiment. We tasked Gemini, ChatGPT, Claude, and Grok with four specific predictions:

  1. Their predicted World Cup champion and why.
  2. Their predicted finalists.
  3. Their predicted semifinalists.
  4. Two or three upset predictions that could shake up the bracket.

No hints, no coaching, just the raw data and their collective intelligence. Here's what they had to say.

Gemini's Brazil Bet: The Most Complete Team

First up was Gemini 3.1 Pro, running in its 'Extended Thinking' mode. Google's advanced general intelligence model didn't hesitate, picking **Brazil** as the 2026 World Cup champion. Its reasoning was strong, citing Vinicius Júnior hitting peak form, Neymar's return from injury, and tactical flexibility across the squad. Gemini called Brazil 'the most complete team entering the knockouts.' For the final, it predicted a clash between Brazil and France, with France being the 'experienced, defensively solid counterweight built for knockout football.' The semifinalists, according to Gemini, would be Argentina and Germany. For upset predictions, Gemini highlighted South Africa's 'historic momentum' after qualifying for the knockouts for the first time, playing with 'house money and zero pressure,' and Mexico finally 'breaking the quinto partido curse' after a flawless nine-point group stage. Morocco was also mentioned as 'tactically equipped to frustrate any possession-heavy European side.'

ChatGPT's French Flair: Balance Over Talent Alone

Next, we turned to ChatGPT's free tier. Right away, we got a different answer. ChatGPT's pick for champion was **France**. The reasoning centered on Kylian Mbappé being 'the most dangerous knockout-stage player in the tournament' and France possessing 'the deepest combination of talent, experience, and athleticism.' A key insight from ChatGPT was France's balance: 'Unlike some contenders that rely heavily on one superstar or one tactical system, France can win a shootout, a defensive battle, or a penalty shootout.' The predicted final was France over Argentina, with Lionel Messi's 'final World Cup run going deep, but not deep enough.' Its semifinalists included Spain and Brazil. But here's where ChatGPT dropped a truly bold prediction: the **United States reaching the semifinals** on home soil, citing a 'favorable bracket path' if they avoid top teams until late. Morocco was again mentioned as an upset pick, as was Norway making a quarterfinal run, led by Haaland. ChatGPT also provided actual win probabilities: France at 18%, Argentina 15%, Spain 13%, and Brazil 12%.

Claude's Calculated Spain Pick: It's All About the Bracket

Claude Opus 4.8, in 'High Effort' mode, approached the task differently. Before giving a single prediction, Claude went to work, searching the web, pulling live betting odds, checking the bracket structure, and analyzing where teams landed in the draw. It identified France as the betting favorite (+400), followed by Spain (+500) and England (+600). Argentina had 'surged into clear second' after 'thrashing Austria.' Claude's pick for champion was **Spain**. The reasoning was fascinating, combining talent with bracket geography: 'Spain is my pick to lift it at MetLife on July 19. The logic is part talent, part bracket geography.' Claude highlighted Spain's status as 'reigning European champions with the best midfield control' and the return of 'Lamine Yamal back and scoring.' Crucially, Claude identified that France and Spain landed in the *same half* of the bracket, meaning 'one of them is going home before the final no matter what.' Spain's quarter was deemed 'softer (a probable QF against the USA),' allowing them to arrive at the semifinal 'fresher and with a cleaner path.' The predicted final was Spain vs. Argentina. Semifinalists included France, Argentina, and England. Upset picks from Claude included Morocco knocking out the Netherlands in the Round of 32, Brazil exiting before the semifinals, and Mexico 'stunning England on home soil.'

"The single most important thing the draw produced is a brutally lopsided bracket, which drives everything below."

Grok's Guarded France Forecast: The Least Decisive

Finally, we turned to Grok 4.3, powered by Elon Musk's xAI. Grok's pick for champion was **France**, aligning with ChatGPT. Its reasoning focused on France's 'strongest overall contender based on squad depth, recent major tournament consistency (finalists in 2018 and 2022), current form, and a generational core of players hitting their prime or peak.' However, Grok was the least decisive of the four AIs, hedging its bets on finalists (France vs. Spain/Argentina) and semifinalists (France, Spain, Argentina, Germany, or England/Brazil). Its upset picks were also more general: 'South Africa or Canada advancing deep,' 'Morocco causing problems for a top side,' and 'a third-place team like Sweden or Paraguay pulling off a surprise.' Grok's vagueness was notable, especially given its powerful infrastructure.

The Verdict: AI's Lack of Consensus (Except for One)

So, what did we learn? Four powerful AI systems, three different champions: Brazil, France, and Spain. There was no consensus on who would win the 2026 World Cup. However, two things did stand out:

  • **Morocco as a Legitimate Upset Threat:** Every single AI, independently and without knowledge of the others' responses, flagged Morocco as a legitimate upset threat. A team that reached the semifinals in 2022 and hasn't been taken seriously enough since.
  • **Argentina Goes Deep:** All four AIs had the defending champions, Argentina, reaching the semifinals or better. Messi's last World Cup run isn't over yet.

Why This Matters to You

These aren't just fun predictions; they are already being tested. The Round of 32 is underway, and teams are either delivering or collapsing. Whether you're a football fan or an AI enthusiast, these real-world tests show us the current capabilities and limitations of our most advanced artificial intelligences.
