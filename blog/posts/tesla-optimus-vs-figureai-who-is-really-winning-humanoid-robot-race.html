Tesla Optimus vs. FigureAI: Who's Really Winning the Humanoid Robot Race?

While Elon Musk paints a grand vision for Optimus, another company is quietly deploying thousands of humanoid robots into real factory jobs right now.

Everyone thinks Tesla is winning the humanoid robot race. But what if we told you that assumption might be completely backwards? Today, we're kicking off a brand new pillar on The AI Lab Report: Physical AI – robots leaving the lab and showing up in the real world. And there's no better place to start than the fight everyone's watching: Tesla's Optimus versus FigureAI.

Tesla’s Vision: Ambition Meets Manufacturing Power

Let's start fair, because Tesla’s case is undeniably real. They've just taped out a brand new chip, AI5, and for the first time, Elon Musk confirmed it's not even going into cars first. Instead, it's being built specifically for Optimus and Tesla’s AI compute clusters. That's a serious signal: they're pivoting silicon around this robot, demonstrating a deep commitment.

The hardware is also real. We're talking about Gen 3 hands with 22 degrees of freedom and 50 actuators – more than double the previous generation. Production lines at Fremont are already being converted for low-volume manufacturing, with plans to start this summer. And let's not forget Tesla’s financial muscle: they’re sitting on over $41 billion in reserve. The ambition here is not hype; Tesla is building for scale in a way almost nobody else in this space can match, aiming for a million robots a year.

FigureAI’s Reality: Robots on the Factory Floor Today

Here's where it gets interesting, though. While Tesla has been building toward the future, FigureAI has been quietly living in the present. Figure’s robots have been doing actual paid work, running real shifts on real production lines at a BMW plant in South Carolina. We’re not talking about a demo or a staged event here. These robots have been doing assembly work for ten months, helping to build over 30,000 vehicles. BMW was so impressed that they just expanded that partnership to a second plant.

Figure is also running something called Helix, an AI system that replaced over 100,000 lines of handwritten balance code with a neural network that simply learns it instead. That’s a real technical leap, not just a marketing claim. And investors have noticed. Figure recently closed a funding round that valued the company at $39 billion, with heavyweights like Nvidia, Microsoft, and Amazon’s Jeff Bezos personally writing checks.

The Uncomfortable Truth: Demos vs. Deployment

Now, here’s the part that’s uncomfortable, but I think it's the whole story, so we’re not skipping it. On Tesla’s own Q4 earnings call, Musk stated that Optimus is “not in usage in our factories in a material way.” That's Tesla, talking about their own robot. Multiple reputable outlets, including Bloomberg, Electrek, TechCrunch, and The Verge, have separately documented that a lot of Optimus demos rely on teleoperation – meaning a human is remotely controlling the robot.

“not in usage in our factories in a material way” - Elon Musk

The most recent example was a demo late last year where a robot tipped over in a way that looked a lot like an operator's headset coming off. To be fair, teleoperation isn't nothing, and every robotics company uses it during development. But there's a real difference between a human helping a robot learn and a human driving it live on stage. That distinction matters, and it’s exactly the kind of gap this channel tries to call out, no matter who it's about.

The Verdict: Who’s Actually Racing?

So, who’s actually ahead? If you're measuring by scale, vision, and long-term ambition, then Tesla is clearly in the lead. Nobody else is building for a million robots a year. But if you're measuring by what’s real, deployed, and making money right now, FigureAI is undeniably out front.

And here’s our honest read: in this industry, right now, real-world deployment matters more. The truly scarce resource in humanoid robotics isn't factory capacity; it's real-world data. Every shift FigureAI runs at BMW teaches their AI something that Tesla’s robots, sitting mostly undeployed, aren't learning yet.

What’s Next: The Race Is Just Beginning

Tesla isn't out of this race, not even close. If Optimus V3 actually ships this summer the way they're promising, this whole conversation could look very different by the end of the year. That's exactly why Physical AI is a new pillar on this channel – not a one-off video. We're going to track this story the same way we track the Musk AI Empire and the Anthropic story: as it actually happens, not just as the press releases want you to see it.

Everyone's been asking who's going to win the humanoid robot race. We don't think that's the right question yet. The right question is: who's actually racing? Right now, that's FigureAI. Tesla is still lacing up its shoes. Whether that changes by the end of the year, that's exactly what we're going to find out together.
